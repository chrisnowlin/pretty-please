Fraction Activities for Grade 3

Activity 1: Paper Folding Fractions

Materials Needed:
- Construction paper (various colors)
- Scissors (optional)
- Crayons or markers

Instructions:
1. Give each student a sheet of construction paper
2. Have them fold the paper in half
3. Ask: "What fraction of the paper is each half?" (Answer: 1/2)
4. Fold again to create fourths
5. Ask: "How many equal parts do we have now?" (Answer: 4 parts, each is 1/4)
6. Have students shade or color different fractions

Activity 2: Fraction Circle Games

Materials Needed:
- Fraction circle manipulatives
- Fraction circle worksheets

Instructions:
1. Distribute fraction circles to students
2. Show them how the circles can be divided into halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, and eighths
3. Practice building different fractions:
   - "Show me 2/4 using your fraction circles"
   - "Show me 3/6 using your fraction circles"
4. Discuss which fractions are the same size (equivalent fractions)

Activity 3: Pizza Party Fractions

Set up a role-play scenario:
- "We have a pizza cut into 8 slices"
- "If you eat 2 slices, what fraction of the pizza did you eat?" (2/8)
- "If your friend eats 3 slices, what fraction did they eat?" (3/8)
- "How many slices are left?" (3 slices, or 3/8)

This makes fractions relevant to real life!

Activity 4: Fraction Art

Materials Needed:
- Graph paper
- Colored pencils
- Rulers

Instructions:
1. Students create designs on graph paper
2. They color different fractions of their design
3. Example: "Color 1/4 of your design red, 1/2 blue, and 1/4 yellow"
4. Students write the fractions they used

Activity 5: Fraction Scavenger Hunt

Create a classroom or home scavenger hunt:
- "Find something that is divided into 2 equal parts"
- "Find something that shows 1/4"
- "Find something you could divide into thirds"

Students learn to see fractions in everyday objects!

Differentiation Strategies:

For Struggling Learners:
- Use larger fraction pieces (halves and fourths only)
- Provide more hands-on manipulatives
- Work in small groups with teacher support
- Use real objects (apples, cookies) for concrete examples

For Advanced Learners:
- Introduce equivalent fractions (1/2 = 2/4)
- Challenge them with fractions like 5/8 or 7/10
- Have them create their own fraction word problems
- Introduce simple addition of fractions with like denominators

Assessment Ideas:

- Exit ticket: Draw and shade a fraction
- Fraction quiz: Identify numerator and denominator
- Performance task: Create a fraction poster
- Observation checklist during hands-on activities
- Fraction journal: Students write about what they learned

These activities make learning fractions fun and engaging for third graders!
